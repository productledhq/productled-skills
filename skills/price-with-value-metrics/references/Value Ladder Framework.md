# Value Ladder Framework

> A product-led business will die if it can't convert users into paying customers.

> Your pricing must align your company's success with your user's success. **When they win, you win.**

## Four Pricing Traps to Avoid

### Trap 1: Not Being Transparent
If you go to a company's site and don't see the price... **it's expensive.** Transparent pricing builds trust, speeds decisions, smooths upgrades.

### Trap 2: Misaligned Incentives
Your incentive: more revenue. User's incentive: maximize value. Great pricing aligns these.

**Symptoms:** flat LTV, flat ARPU.

> Paddle.com case: charge by features → Walmart.com signs up at $99/mo and processes $100M in month one. Team is buried. **Charge a % of revenue processed instead** (3–5%, like a credit card), which scales with the value they receive.

### Trap 3: Big Jumps in Pricing
Free → $2,000/mo is too steep. Most companies with steep jumps don't have a metric that scales well. Start at $50; get to $2,000 over time.

### Trap 4: Confusing Pricing Page
If users can't pick the right plan in **<5 seconds**, your page needs work.

## Phase 1: Identify Your [[Value Metric|Value Metrics]]

> A **value metric** = the way you measure value exchange in your product.

| Company | Value metric |
|---|---|
| Vidyard | Number of videos uploaded |
| Slack | Number of users |
| Paddle | Total revenue generated |

> Patrick Campbell (ProfitWell): value metrics outperform feature differentiation with up to **75% less churn.**

### Great Value Metric Criteria
- **Simple**: users immediately understand what they're paying for
- **Aligned** with the product's core value (often = your [[Key Usage Indicator (KUI)|KUI]])
- **Scalable**: fair to charge more at higher usage

### Senja Example

| Metric | Simple? | Aligned? | Scalable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| # of users | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ |
| # of testimonials collected | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |

Cap at **1–2 value metrics.** Less is more.

If you're in an established market, **do a market scan** (Greg Leach). The value metric is likely well-defined; benefit from convention.

## Phase 2: Build Your Pricing Matrix

The DEEP Model (internal) → pricing matrix (external).

**PromoTix sample:**

| | Ticketing | Ticketing + Marketing | Professional |
|---|---|---|---|
| **For** | Events that want a robust ticketing solution | Events that want additional marketing tools | Professional venues, large festivals and events |
| **Price** | Free | $/month | $$/month |
| **Includes** | 1.75% + $1.19 per ticket · Free for organizers passing fees to attendees · Unlimited ticket types · Unlimited events | Everything in Free · Google/Facebook/AdRoll tracking pixels · Mailchimp email integration | Everything in Ticketing+Marketing · No ticketing fees · RFID capabilities · Mobile app CSS · Ambassador network access |
| **CTA** | Get Started | Get Started | Get Started |

### Step 1: Name Your Plans
Generic > confusing. Common: Basic/Pro/Advanced · Free/Starter/Professional · Starter/Business/Enterprise. **Even better: function-based** (Ticketing / Ticketing + Marketing / Professional).

### Step 2: Positioning One-Liner
Two things:
1. Who this plan is for
2. What it enables them to do

> Without this, you fail the **5-second skim test.**

### Step 3: Define What's Included
- How much value metric per plan
- ≤5 features per plan (link to a comparison chart for full breakdown)
- Make value metric the first feature for quick scanning

For non-obvious features, use **tooltips** on the page.

### Step 4: Call-to-Action
Almost always "Get Started For Free" / "Try For Free" / "Start Free Trial." Most popular plan (usually middle) gets high-contrast CTA.

## Phase 3: Determine the Ideal Price

> Use the **Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter** ("Van West Model"). Same model used by ProfitWell, OpenView, Simon-Kucher.

Avoid two disasters:
1. Too high → lose most sales
2. Too low → lose most profits + look cheap

### Step 1: Choose ONE Plan
Most PLG companies start with the **intermediate plan** (most paid signups).

### Step 2: Prep the Survey

> **ChatGPT didn't call it a pricing study.** They offered a waitlist for "ChatGPT Professional" (fast/no-blackouts/2× message limit) and slipped the Van West questions in.

**Full version (5 questions):**
1. Email (so they can dig into usage)
2. Country (willingness to pay varies by country)
3. Most valuable use case
4. **At what price is it "expensive" (you'd have to think twice)?**
5. **At what price is it "acceptable" (good value for money)?**

Optional add-on (Sean Ellis): *"How upset would you be if you could no longer use [product]?"* (1–5): segments raving fans from casual.

**Kyle Poyar's 2-question version** (use if low customer count):
1. What's an "acceptable" price?
2. When does it seem "expensive"?

### Step 3: Who to Ask
- To price intermediate → ask **ideal free users**
- To price advanced → ask **intermediate plan customers**

> Your ideal users might value the product at **10× the amount non-ideal users do.** Jumble the data and you miss landslide profits.

Drive traffic: in-app notifications · manual emails · email asking for participation.

### Step 4: Decide

Plot X-axis (prices) × Y-axis (% selecting that range). The intersection of "Acceptable" and "Too Expensive" = **Optimal Price Point.**

## Phase 4: Build a Simple Pricing Page

Three required components:

### 1. Anchor Tagline
Present a higher anchor before showing your price.
- vs. a sales rep cost (ProductLed)
- vs. a competitor's overall cost

> ProductLed mentions hiring a sales rep costs more than a ProductLed Implementer right before showing pricing. The investment then feels like a no-brainer.

### 2. Pricing Matrix
With monthly/annual toggle.

### 3. FAQ
- Everyone asks → put in main copy
- Some ask → FAQ
- Few ask → cut it
- Use a chat bubble for the rest

### Optional Enhancements
- **Why choose your product**: healthy redundancy reassuring buying decision
- **Risk Reversal**: address the biggest risk head-on
- **Social Proof**: ≥3 pieces tied to top 3 objections
