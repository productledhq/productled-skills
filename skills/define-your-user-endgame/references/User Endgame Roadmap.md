# User Endgame Roadmap

> **Whoever knows the customer best wins the market.**

Three phases:
1. **Identify** your ideal user
2. **Clarify** their endgame
3. **Craft** a user roadmap

## Phase 1: Identify Your Ideal User

### What Makes a User "Ideal"
1. **Loves your product**: even with current rough edges
2. **Great retention**: sticks around, gains substantial value
3. **Highly successful**: derives more value than other users
4. **Strategic**: helps you break into the market

### Step 1: List 5–10 potential ideal users
B2B = usually job titles. Remember: **buyer ≠ user** (the VP of Demand Gen buys; the Digital Marketing Specialist uses).

### Step 2: Score on the BJ Fogg Behavior Model

| Dimension | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Motivation** | Something is pushing or pulling them |
| **Ability** | Easy for them to pick up (e.g., programming experience) |
| **Prompts** | Top-of-mind, constant triggers |

Optional add-on columns: TAM · free-to-paid rate · retention.

> **Kloudle's counter-intuitive pick:** security engineers were most motivated, highest ability, strongest prompts, but saturated. Founder Akash picked **developers at smaller companies** instead. Less motivated, but Kloudle's strength was *automatically* fixing security issues without a security engineer. Match the user to your strength, not the most obvious motivator.

### Step 3: Pick ONE

> **Colin Powell's 40/70 rule:** with <40% of info you'll make a bad choice. With >70% you waited too long.

Use three brains:
- **Head**: who do I *think* is right? (logic + data)
- **Heart**: who do I *feel* is right? (intuition)
- **Gut**: who do I have the *courage* to commit to?

*(Read mBraining by Soosalu & Oka for the science.)*

> ProductLed first picked senior product execs. Eventually realized PLG only works when adopted by the CEO. Moved to founders/CEOs. **Your ideal user evolves.**

## Phase 2: Clarify the Endgame

What is a user *capable of* once they experience your product's value? *(Mario with a flower.)*

### Step 1: List All Core Outcomes
Ask three questions:
- What will users see when successful?
- What will they feel?
- What will they hear?

ProductLed: [[Effortless ARR]] · [[Lean Scale]] · [[Durable Growth]]

### Step 2: Pinpoint Top 3
Typical buckets:
- **Fix**: immediate pain
- **Prevent**: fear of missing out / harm
- **Improve**: long-term gain

### Step 3: Define User Endgame Statement

| Company | Endgame |
|---|---|
| Canva | Design anything and publish anywhere |
| Hotjar | Get insights to create digital experiences your users love |
| Google | Find relevant answers to any question |
| Shopify | Build your dream business |
| Zapier | Automate your busy work |

Criteria:
- Communicates what it's not
- Inspires
- Simple
- Achievable
- Your product can deliver on it

## Phase 3: Craft a User Roadmap

The exact steps a user must take to get value. **5 stages, 10 milestones.**

### Search Stage

| Milestone | What |
|---|---|
| Problem Aware | They realize they have a problem |
| Active Research | They research how to solve it |

Common challenges: information overload, fear of change, complacency, limited knowledge, cost concerns, technical jargon, integration concerns, security/privacy.

> Educate users on the **root problem** before they solve the wrong problem.

### Select Stage

| Milestone | What |
|---|---|
| Visit | Land on your website |
| Sign Up | Sign up for your free product |

Visit challenges: nav issues, too many CTAs, slow loading, unresponsive design, unclear value prop, lack of trust indicators.
Signup challenges: unclear benefits, too many form fields, technical issues, mandatory credit card, privacy concerns, uncertainty about trial/freemium limits, confusing pricing.

Screen recording tools (Hotjar) help here.

### Setup Stage

> The clock is ticking. Users have finite energy, time, motivation.

| Milestone | What |
|---|---|
| **Profile** | Few questions to accelerate onboarding |
| **Onboarding** | Walk them through what they need to do |

> **Wave (financial management):** asks a few business + goal questions at signup → customizes dashboard + surfaces relevant features.

Common challenges: survey fatigue, unclear purpose, privacy, language/accessibility, no immediate benefit, complex installation, unclear instructions, overwhelming features, time-consuming, insufficient support.

Most companies lose **40–60% of signups** here.

### Showcase Stage

| Milestone | What |
|---|---|
| **[[First Strike]]** | Experience core value for the FIRST time |
| **[[Key Usage Indicator (KUI)\|KUI]]** | Experience core value multiple times |

**First Strike examples:**
- Create your first graphic in Canva
- Have your first call on Zoom
- Watch your first movie on Netflix
- Play your first chess game on Chess.com

> A great First Strike delivers significant value while leaving room for further discovery. **Not the entire value.** Enough to motivate return.

**KUI** = the *come-back-for-more* signal. Often = First Strike experienced 3+ times.
- Slack: account reaches 2,000-message limit
- Facebook: 7 friends added

KUIs are the **leading indicator of who's likely to upgrade**, and the foundation of [[Product Qualified Lead (PQL)|PQLs]].

Three challenge buckets:
- **Product Gap**: UX/onboarding friction
- **Knowledge Gap**: high learning curve
- **Skill Gap**: capability not yet developed

### Scale Stage

| Milestone | What |
|---|---|
| Upgrade | User pays |
| Advance | Grows out of plan / uses solution more |

> Take "upgrade" literally. **What is the user looking to upgrade in their *life*?** Not features. Solve a genuine problem they care about. Sequential.

> **Senja:** free plan brands testimonials "Powered by Senja." As businesses become more established, they want professional → upgrade removes the brand.

**Advance** = continued value justifies the upgrade. In Canva/Miro/Notion the product becomes part of the workflow. For others, users complete everything they need.

Some users **graduate** to more advanced solutions. Others collaborate with you to build out advanced features.

Common challenges:
- Upgrade: confusing pricing, inadequate free experience, payment issues, unclear cancellation, limited payment options, migration concerns
- Advance: steep learning curve, prohibitive pricing, integration difficulties, inadequate customization, performance issues, lack of advanced tutorials, team adoption
