# DEEP Model

> Design an intentional product-led model where your product's true value shines in the free experience.

**Four phases (the acronym):**

| Letter | Phase | What |
|---|---|---|
| **D** | Desirable | Gamify your model into three levels |
| **E** | Effective | Unpack the biggest challenges |
| **E** | Efficient | Decide what to give away vs. gate |
| **P** | Polish | Pick the free model + package as one-pager |

## Phase 1: Desirable: Gamify Into Three Levels

Every game has levels: beginner / intermediate / advanced.

### The Cold Start Problem

> Starting to solve advanced problems in your free motion before addressing beginner problems.

**Vidyard's original free trial** required users to:
1. Upload a video
2. Embed it on a website
3. Integrate with marketing automation
4. Drive traffic
5. See who's watching

Most users didn't even have a video to upload. Result: trial bombed.

**The fix:** a simple Chrome extension that made it easy to record. Beginner problem solved. **100K+ users in 12 months, millions since.**

### Three Levels

| Level | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Beginner** | A problem everyone in your market would need help with (*this is what you give away*) |
| **Intermediate** | A problem most will eventually struggle with |
| **Advanced** | A problem only a minority struggles with |

> **Vidyard:** Start recording videos easily → Unlock unlimited videos + sales → Level up your entire team's video sales game
>
> **PromoTix:** Publish an event → Sell more tickets with marketing tools → Save big on ticketing fees

### Beginner Outcome Must Be

- **Unique**: your product's unique core value shines
- **Desirable**: your ideal user really wants this outcome
- **Effective**: they can experience it in **7 minutes or less**

> No one likes to play a game they can't win. If you can't make your free offer desirable, no one will want to play.

> Be generous with beginner level. **Most companies give away too little.** *(Coaching platform that wanted to give away 5 free courses; companies took up to a year to set up 5 → recommended 1 free course instead.)*

## Phase 2: Effective: Unpack the Biggest Challenges

Review your [[User Endgame Roadmap]]. Place each user challenge into its level.

For each level, identify 5–10 challenges across three buckets:
1. **Product challenges**: how can users experience the value?
2. **Knowledge challenges**: what do they not know that holds them back?
3. **Skill challenges**: what lacking skills prevent success?

> **Userguiding.com**: product is simple to set up. But users need a lot of education on **good user onboarding strategy**. Many challenges are knowledge + skill, not product.

> AI brainstorming prompt: *"List out the biggest challenges for [Ideal User] to achieve [Desired Outcome]."*

### Prioritize: Roadblock Rating (1–5)

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Barely a challenge |
| 2 | Small (almost no one struggles) |
| 3 | Decent (many users point it out) |
| 4 | Most face it but can't overcome |
| 5 | No one can overcome without support |

**Shortlist the top 3** per level. Don't ignore the rest. Just focus.

## Phase 3: Efficient: Decide What to Give Away vs. Gate

### Step 1: The PCR Test

For each top challenge, find 5–10 solutions across three types:

- **P**roduct solutions (features)
- **C**ontent solutions (blog posts, guides, examples)
- **R**esource solutions (interactive tools, quizzes, lists)

> Why so many? You'll find creative solutions. And the brainstorming bias: it's too easy to focus only on product solutions. **Sometimes a blog post solves what a feature can't.**

### Step 2: Prioritize on Impact × Cost

For each solution, score **Impact** (helping user overcome) and **Cost** (business to roll out).

| Impact ↓ / Cost → | Low | High |
|---|---|---|
| **High** | 🟢 **Pursue** | 🤝 **White Glove** *(learn fast, won't scale)* |
| **Low** | ⚡ **Quick Wins** | ❌ **Avoid** |

> Make every solution fight for its life. Low-cost, high-impact = rare. Expect to kill most.

Every team contributes: marketing → content · sales → enablement resources · etc.

### Step 3: Shortlist

Top 3 challenges + top 3–5 solutions per level. **First time: focus on Beginner only.**

For usage-based products, also list how many units to give away (5 seats, 3 boards, 5 courses).

### The Free Model Foundation

- Give away everything at beginner
- Intermediate = first paid plan
- Advanced = charge the most + grow with customers

## Phase 4: Polish: Pick the Right Free Model

Six options. See full breakdown: [[Six Product-Led Models]].

1. **Opt-in Free Trial**: time-based, no credit card
2. **Opt-out Free Trial**: credit card required *(last resort)*
3. **Usage-Based Free Trial**: limit by units (Miro: 3 boards · Vidyard: 25 videos · Evernote: 50 notes)
4. **Freemium**: forever-free for some features (Canva)
5. **New Product**: when no existing model bridges the value gap (Vidyard Chrome extension)
6. **Sandbox**: clickable tour with dummy data *(last resort, complements other models)*

> **Pick ONE** if first time. Don't be cute with hybrid models until you've mastered basics.

> **Rob Walling** (formerly Drip): "Freemium is like a Samurai sword: unless you're a master at using it, you can cut your arm off."

### The One-Pager Output

- Desired Outcome
- Model (one selected)
- Top 3 challenges your ideal user will encounter
- Solutions you're giving away
- (Optional) What's included in future versions?

> Don't limit your offer to behind-the-scenes functionality. Pitch "everything they need to publish their first event," not "an event page to help sell tickets."
