# Define Your User Endgame

Products don't retain users; transformations do. The User Endgame is the specific, observable "after" state your ideal user reaches, and every downstream decision (offer, onboarding, pricing, metrics) inherits from it. If it's fuzzy, everything downstream is fuzzy.

## How to work

1. **Get the raw material**: who uses the product today, which segment gets the most value fastest, what users say in their own words when the product "clicks," and what the user's life/work looks like after 90 days of successful use.
2. **Read `references/User Endgame Roadmap.md`** and run it as an exercise: narrow to the ideal user, then articulate their endgame as a transformation, not a feature outcome ("runs payroll in 10 minutes a month", not "uses our payroll module").
3. **Apply the DEEP bar** (`references/DEEP Model.md`) to the endgame statement and rewrite until it passes.
4. **Produce the artifact** from `references/user-endgame.md`: the deliverable is the filled template, not advice about filling it.

## Output format

The completed User Endgame artifact: ideal user (specific enough to exclude people), the endgame transformation, the milestones between signup and endgame, and the first observable moment of value. Every line quantified where possible: "faster reporting" is banned; "monthly close in 2 days instead of 9" is the bar.
