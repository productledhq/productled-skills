# Choose Your PLG Model

"Free trial or freemium?" is the wrong first question. The right one: **how much proof does your buyer need before they believe, and how cheaply can you deliver it?** The model falls out of that answer.

## How to work

1. **Get the inputs**: what the product does, who buys it, price point, time-to-value today (minutes? days?), cost to serve a free user, and how skeptical the market is (new category vs. established).
2. **Read `references/PLG Spectrum.md`** and place them on the spectrum: how much of the product to give away, and for how long, given their Cost of Proof.
3. **Read `references/Six Product-Led Models.md`** and map their situation to a model. Don't present all six. Pick one, name the runner-up, and say why the others lose.
4. **Pressure-test with time-to-value** (`references/Time-to-Value.md`): a model that requires two weeks of setup can't run on a 14-day trial. If TTV is too long for any model to work, say so. That's a product problem, not a packaging problem.

## Output format

1. **The model**: one committed recommendation with the two strongest reasons.
2. **What's free and what's paid**: the specific line, drawn around their product's features, not generic advice.
3. **The runner-up**: and the evidence that would make you switch to it.
4. **First experiment**: the smallest test that validates the model within 30 days, with the metric to watch.
