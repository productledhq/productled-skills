# Obvious Choice

> **The market default.** When someone needs to schedule a meeting → Calendly. Messaging → Slack. Design → Canva. Video → Zoom.

## Equivalent in Book 01

The "Obvious Choice" maps directly to **[[Market Ascent (5 Levels)|Chosen]]** in [[Product-Led Growth (2nd Edition)|Book 01]]'s Market Ascent.

## The Defining Feature

> When you reach Chosen status, you experience **automatic profits.** The market sends customers to you without your team chasing them.

You can afford to build an **ace team**: the best people want to work for the market leader. And you have **absolute leverage**, because your product handles the heavy lifting while marketing punches above its weight through sheer word of mouth.

## The Myth and the Truth

| Myth | Truth |
|---|---|
| The best products win | The companies that **win the majority of the market** win |

You can build the best product, fastest onboarding, cleanest design, highest activation, and still not be the obvious choice. Chosen requires **best product + marketing engine to capture majority share.** One without the other isn't enough.

## The Riches Are in the Niches

> "I could never become the obvious choice in my market."

You don't need to beat HubSpot at all CRM. **Be the best CRM for real estate agents.** Big markets have niches, each with its own obvious choice.

> The founder who thinks "I can't compete with HubSpot" is asking the wrong question. The right question: **"Can I become the obvious choice for a specific segment HubSpot underserves?"**

## Why It Compounds (Crossing the Chasm)

Geoffrey A. Moore (*Crossing the Chasm*): cross from early adopters (risk-takers) to early majority (cautious pragmatists) by getting current users to **recommend you.**

> Obvious-choice products don't just cross the chasm faster. **They win innovators, early adopters, late majority, and laggards.**

**Laggards** are risk-averse: they just want to use what everyone else is using. *"Nobody gets fired for choosing Microsoft."*

Once you're the obvious choice:
- Users recommend your product even if competitors offer something slightly better
- People stop searching for alternatives: they stick with what they know
- Free advertising → more profits → more investment in product + talent → harder to copy

## Not Permanent

> Stop deepening the moat → a hungry competitor closes the gap.

To stay here:
- Keep deepening your [[15 Moats|moats]]
- Keep investing in the product
- Keep expanding the market

> The moment you coast, a hungry competitor starts closing the gap.
