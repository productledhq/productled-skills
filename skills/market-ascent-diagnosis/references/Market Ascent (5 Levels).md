# Market Ascent (5 Levels)

> Every product-led business falls into one of five competitive positions. **Companies move up. Companies slide back. Most stall at a level they could have moved past, because they didn't know what the next level required.**

The Market Ascent is the **spine of the [[Product Lab Library]] series**: Books 03–06 are each planned around one of the climbs.

## Level 1: Commodity
*Your product isn't compelling enough for prospects to care.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Feels like** | Tiny profits (price competition), treading water, tiresome customers needing hand-holding |
| **Myth** | "Running a business is just a tough slog." |
| **Truth** | The product must do the heavy lifting. Your job is **product-market fit**. Make your difference impossible to miss. |
| **Why stuck** | Lack of knowledge, not talent: never seen what working PLG looks like |
| **Image** | A Patagonia performance shirt in a $5 t-shirt bin at a garage sale |

Innovative ≠ compelling to the market. Some of the most innovative companies are Commodities because the **positioning** strips away what makes them special.

---

## Level 2: Contender
*You have a real product, but growth depends on effort, not pull.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Feels like** | Sporadic revenue (hundreds of K to a couple of M), unpredictable pipeline, real traction in pockets |
| **Myth** | "You need to be everything to everyone." |
| **Truth** | You need a winning GTM motion + focus. Turn a differentiated product into one that sells itself. |
| **Trap** | Differentiated ≠ hard to copy |

### The Tally vs. low-code-builder contrast
- **Tally.so** offers unlimited free forms. Typeform can't match without cannibalizing massive ARR. **Strategy that's structurally hard to copy.**
- **Low-code app builder** ("easiest way to build apps quickly") was undercut overnight by Lovable. Differentiator built on speed. Someone faster showed up.

> Speed without differentiation burns cash. Differentiation without speed gives competitors time to respond. **You need both.**

*The Product-Led Playbook* was written for this stage.

## Level 3: Cusp
*One of the top companies in your market, but not the leader.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **ARR** | Typically $5M–$10M |
| **Feels like** | Predictable revenue, prolific performers, real margins; still duking it with a few players; features copied in months |
| **Myth** | "You need to hire a big team to grow." |
| **Truth** | Design a business that runs itself. **Systems over intuition.** |
| **Job shift** | Contender: design a *product* that sells itself. Cusp: design a *business* that runs itself. |

### The Esben (Userflow) benchmark
$0 → $4.6M ARR with **3 people, no contractors.** >$1.6M revenue per employee. Sold for **8x revenue multiple** in ~3 years. *Not every founder needs to climb past this level.*

### The "good enough" barrier
This is the level that kills more potential than any other. Profits + good team + predictable revenue feels safe. **Staying still isn't safe.** A hungrier competitor takes the leap you wouldn't and captures the market position you could have had.

## Level 4: Chosen
*You become the obvious choice in your market.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Examples** | Calendly (scheduling), Slack (messaging), Zoom (video) |
| **What it gives you** | Automatic profits, ace team (best people want to work for the leader), absolute leverage |
| **Myth** | "The best products win." |
| **Truth** | The companies that win the majority of the market win. Best product + marketing engine to capture majority share. Both required. |
| **Way in** | The riches are in the niches |

### Niche example
Don't try to beat HubSpot. Become the obvious choice in **real-estate-agent CRM** while HubSpot owns the broad market. Big markets have niches with their own obvious choices.

### Not permanent
Stop deepening the moat → a hungry competitor closes the gap.

---

## Level 5: Crusader
*You stand for something bigger than your product.*

**Defining characteristic: leading with [[Generosity Moat|generosity so extreme it becomes your moat]].**

### Canva
Vision: democratize design. Outrageously generous free plan (core features, thousands of templates, collaboration). Millions create + share daily without paying. **Any competitor would have to match Canva's generosity at Canva's scale without Canva's decade of revenue.** Near-impossible entry.

### Insight Timer
**59% of wellbeing app usage**: more time than Calm + Headspace combined. Generous free → more content creators → more value → more word of mouth. Free plan **is** the moat. Calm and Headspace can't match (no margins).

> You can replicate a feature or match a price. **You can't replicate genuine purpose.**

Not every founder will reach this. That's fine. But every founder should know it exists.
