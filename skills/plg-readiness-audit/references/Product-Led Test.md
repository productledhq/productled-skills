# The Product-Led Test

> Giving something away for free **does not make you product-led.** A PDF that gates an email and teaches nothing is *marketing with a gate*, not PLG.

The test has two parts. You must pass both.

## Part 1: The Value Test

> **If you charged for your free experience, would people still pay for it?**

| Answer | What you're running |
|---|---|
| **No** | Lead-generation play (lead magnet) |
| **Yes** | [[Proof Experience]]: something close to product-led |

When someone goes through a real Proof Experience, they walk away thinking *"that alone was worth paying for."* They don't feel funneled or teased. **They feel like they've already gotten something meaningful, and now they want more.**

### Canva example
The Proof Experience isn't browsing templates. It's **actually designing something with the platform** and producing a high-quality design. By design.

## Part 2: The Economics Test

> **Can you deliver that proof at a cost that makes sense for your business?**

This is where [[Cost of Proof]] enters.

- SaaS with ~$0 Cost of Proof → offer the proof experience to everyone
- Consulting firm → qualify first, then deliver proof
- **Both can be product-led.** The economics just look different.

If your Cost of Proof is astronomical, PLG isn't your move *right now*. (See: [[Cost of Proof]] for why "right now" matters. AI may be lowering it faster than you think.)

## Passing the Test

> If someone can experience your value before they pay, **and** you can deliver that proof at a cost that makes sense, **the frameworks in this book apply to you**, whether you run a service business, marketplace, education platform, or SaaS company.

## Common Failure Modes

1. **Lead magnet pretending to be PLG.** Free ebook that just collects emails. Fails Part 1.
2. **Generous freemium that bleeds cash.** Passes Part 1 (people love it) but fails Part 2 (you can't sustain it).
3. **"Free trial" no one can activate.** Fails Part 1: there's no real proof, just access.
