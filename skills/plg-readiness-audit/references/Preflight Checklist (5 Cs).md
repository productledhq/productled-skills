# Preflight Checklist: The 5 Cs

> Most PLG failures don't happen because the strategy was wrong. They happen because the company **wasn't ready**.

Five levels. Each builds on the one below. Skipping levels is how launches crash.

```
                   ▲
              5. Cash             ← Is PLG becoming your business?
              4. Construction     ← Does your product overdeliver?
              3. Capabilities     ← Does your team have the skills?
              2. Commitment       ← Is leadership backing it with resources + patience?
              1. Conviction       ← Do we believe this is right?
                   ▼
                FOUNDATION
```

---

## 1. Conviction (personal)
> Do we believe PLG is right for our market and vision?

The #1 differentiator across hundreds of customers wasn't budget, team size, or product maturity. It was **whether the CEO and founding team truly believed PLG was the way forward.**

### Checks
- Is PLG the **only way** to your vision, or one option?
- Can you articulate why PLG is right **without referencing competitors**?
- Would you keep going if results took **18 months**?
- 1–10 belief. **Below 7 → don't proceed.**

> Conviction is the fuel in the tank.

---

## 2. Commitment (organizational)
> Is leadership willing to back PLG with real resources and real patience?

The real-estate SaaS CEO who bombed PLG: smart hire, all-in CEO, but no one else on the leadership team bought in. Came back three years later, same strategy, different result, because the whole team was committed.

### Checks
- Does **every** leader get it, want it, and have capacity?
- Is PLG a **top-3 priority**? (Not top-10. Not "on the roadmap.")
- **Do incentives reward PLG?** *The silent killer.* People follow paychecks, not mission statements.
- Are you willing to **change the culture**?
- 1–10 commitment. **Below 7 → have the hard conversation. Don't proceed.**

---

## 3. Capabilities (technique)
> Does your team have the skills to build a world-class PLG business?

PLG is a new sport. First practice is humbling. The companies that win **understand they're developing a skill, not flipping a switch.**

The junior UX example: he designed for *looks*, not for *getting users to value as fast as possible*. Trained on the right distinction → unstoppable. **Talent was there; technique wasn't.**

### Checks
- Could your team have an **informed debate** about PLG decisions?
- Right **skills**? UX for time-to-value, data for activation milestones, marketing for cheap self-serve signups.
- Right **knowledge**? Can they define your activation metric *right now*: specific behavior, not vague "value"?
- Core capabilities covered? Product marketing, data analytics, UX, onboarding design, pricing strategy, product analytics, customer research, CRO.
- 1–10. **Most fixable level.** Conviction and Commitment need willpower. Capabilities just need investment.

> The worst thing you can do is rush in with an untrained team. The failure won't *look* like a capabilities failure. It'll look like a strategic failure. And you'll abandon a strategy that would have worked.

---

## 4. Construction (the product)
> Does your product overdeliver on value?

[[Value Gap]] = promised value − experienced value. The bigger the gap, the leakier the funnel. 40–60% of signups never come back.

**Three components of overdelivery:**
1. **Quality** of outcome
2. **Speed** to get it
3. **Price** they pay

Nail one → competitive. Nail all three → people can't stop talking (Canva).

### The new speed bar
- 2019: an hour or two
- 2026: **under 60 seconds.** ChatGPT in seconds. Lovable builds a working app in <1 minute.

Users aren't comparing your product to your competitors'. They're comparing it to the fastest product they've ever used.

### Checks
- "That was fine" vs. "**Wow, that's amazing**": which reaction?
- **Time it.** Sign up as a new user, count seconds to a meaningful outcome.
- Could the user get the same outcome elsewhere for less money / effort?
- 1–10. Most people score <7. **Expected.** Hardest level. *The Product-Led Playbook exists for this.*

---

## 5. Cash (revenue proof)
> Is PLG driving the majority of your revenue?

Day one: self-serve might be 1% while sales carries 99%. **Normal.** The journey: 1% → "becoming substantial" → "majority of business."

> Even if you nail the first four Cs, if PLG never generates real cash, it won't stick.

### Checks
- Does PLG drive the **majority**, or impact >50%?
- Is PLG revenue **growing every month**, or flat? Flat for 3+ months = something in the checklist is broken.
- Does PLG revenue include **sales-assisted deals the product created demand for**? Yes, count it.

### Reading the score
| Score | State |
|---|---|
| <4 | Expected early on. Watch monthly trend. |
| **4–7** | **Danger zone.** Good enough to stay alive, not good enough to be permanent. Don't coast. Install weekly experiment rhythm. |
| >7 | PLG is your engine. Protect and compound. |

---

## Reading the Pyramid

> Find your **lowest** number. That's your bottleneck.

| Lowest C | Action |
|---|---|
| Conviction | Get on board with whether PLG is critical to your vision. |
| Commitment | Stop building. Get leadership behind it. |
| Capabilities | Invest in the team before the product. Common language first. |
| Construction | Get *The Product-Led Playbook*. Follow it in order. |
| Cash | Data system → growth process → keep leveling up the team. |

> The companies that build from the bottom up and refuse to skip levels end up in the top 1%. The rest are still wondering why their free trial isn't working.
