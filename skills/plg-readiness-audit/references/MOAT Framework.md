# MOAT Framework

> Answers *when* and *how*, not *if*, your business should go product-led.

Four dimensions. Each gets a flag: 🟢 / 🟡 / 🔴.

| | Dimension | Question |
|---|---|---|
| **M** | Market Strategy | Which market are you going after? |
| **O** | Ocean Conditions | Red ocean or blue ocean? |
| **A** | Adoption | How easily can someone experience your value? |
| **T** | Touch-to-Value | Does getting users to value require your team? |

---

## M: Market Strategy

The filter companies get wrong most often. They want PLG to work so badly they ignore what the market is telling them.

| Flag | Market | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 🟢 | SMB / Mid-market | PLG is the natural motion. SMB buyers won't sit through a demo for $50/mo. Paying expensive reps to close small deals never math-works. |
| 🟡 | Mid-market with significant enterprise | PLG works as **hybrid**. Product drives adoption, education, qualification. Sales steps in when complexity or contract size justifies. |
| 🔴 | Pure enterprise | PLG isn't your primary motion. Use product-led *elements* to support sales, but don't build strategy around self-serve. |

> ⚠️ This is the only **structural** red flag in MOAT. The other three can be addressed over time.

## O: Ocean Conditions

Blue ocean (create demand) vs. red ocean (compete for existing demand).

### Red Ocean → 🟢
Prospects already understand the problem. PLG widens the funnel, lowers CAC, accelerates global expansion. PLG converts "non-customers": people who'd never request pricing or book a demo, but will *try*.

> Live-chat: started sales-led. Once the category matured, it became almost impossible to find a live-chat app without PLG. **In a red ocean, PLG isn't optional. It's how you survive.**

### Blue Ocean → 🟡
Steep learning curve. Need to educate the market before the product can sell itself. Most companies start sales-led or marketing-led.

**Exception:** if the product teaches users *by being used* (Notion, ChatGPT), you can still lead with PLG in a blue ocean.

> Snowflake had to spend time educating buyers on what a cloud data warehouse was. People don't buy what they don't understand.

### No red flag
All blue oceans become red. Companies with PLG already built will have a massive head start.

> Esben Friis-Jensen (Userflow): *"Going into a red ocean is something I recommend any founder should do. Blue oceans are so much harder."*

## A: Adoption

How many hurdles must a user clear to see value?

### Three layers
1. **Product adoption**: sign up, navigate core workflow, complete a meaningful action.
2. **Knowledge adoption**: understand something new before the product is useful.
3. **Skill adoption**: develop an actual capability to succeed.

| Flag | Layers required | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 🟢 | Product only | Notion, Slack, Zoom |
| 🟡 | Product + knowledge OR skill | Salesforce (CRM is easy to navigate; sales process knowledge required) |
| 🔴 | All three heavy | Photoshop; mission-critical infrastructure |

### The Yellow Flag Trap
Founders underestimate yellows because they have years of context their users don't. They look at their product and think "anyone can figure this out." **Smart founders underestimate this constantly.**

> ProductLed's own example: people need to understand what PLG is, the right approach, frameworks, order of operations. Tons of knowledge. Real skills. *That's why ProductLed gives away its books for free, to close the knowledge gap before someone signs up.*

## T: Touch-to-Value

How much organizational involvement is required before someone experiences value?

| Flag | Touch | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 🟢 | **No-touch**: value and upgrade alone | Canva, Grammarly |
| 🟡 | **Low-touch**: automated mostly; team for edge cases, expansion, high-value accounts | Slack |
| 🔴 | **High-touch**: CSM/Implementation Specialist/Sales Engineer required before value | Enterprise SaaS with complex integrations |

### High-touch as a *learning strategy*
Alex Turnbull (Groove → Helply) personally onboarded every customer in year one, **on purpose**. Every conversation revealed friction, use cases, objections. High-touch should be a deliberate learning phase you work to eliminate, not a permanent state.

### Reducing touch over time
Every improvement to onboarding, smarter defaults, in-app education, automated workflows = less human involvement. **One of the highest-leverage investments a PLG company can make.**

---

## Compounding Flags

A yellow alone is manageable. **Yellows reinforce each other.**

- Yellow Ocean + Yellow Adoption = asking users to educate themselves about a new category **and** develop new skills at the same time. Doesn't scale.

> Watch for combinations, not just individual scores.

---

## Reading the Score

| Pattern | Move |
|---|---|
| **All green** | PLG makes sense. Don't overthink it. Focus on how to start. |
| **One or more yellows** | PLG can work. Address each yellow first: education paired with the product (yellow Ocean) · guided onboarding / AI agents (yellow Adoption) · automate path to value (yellow Touch). *If yellows are stacking, slow down and build foundation first.* |
| **Enterprise only** | PLG probably isn't your path. Use product-led elements to support sales/expansion. |

> No matter where you score, PLG will become your future. Even enterprise will adopt product-led elements as buyers demand proof.
