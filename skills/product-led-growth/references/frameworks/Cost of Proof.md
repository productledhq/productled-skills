# Cost of Proof

> **The real line between PLG-viable and not isn't SaaS vs. non-SaaS. It's the Cost of Proof.**

## Definition

> **Cost of Proof** = how much it costs you to let someone experience real value before they pay.

The lower your Cost of Proof, the faster you can scale a PLG motion. The higher it is, the more deliberate you must be about *who* gets the proof experience and *how* you deliver it.

## The Spectrum

| Business | Cost of Proof | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Software (free trial / freemium) | **~$0**: flip a switch, millions can try tonight | Pure PLG works trivially |
| Airport cologne counter | **Pennies**: a spritz might close a $150 sale | Naturally PLG without anyone calling it that |
| $1,000 jacket | **Real**: inventory, floor space, salesperson | PLG-viable but with friction |
| HIPAA enterprise SaaS | **High**: human walk-through before value | PLG, but must qualify before delivering proof |
| Industrial equipment | **Very high**: physical demos, custom configuration | Likely sales-led until tech changes the math |

## AI Is Collapsing Cost of Proof

In categories where it was prohibitively expensive five years ago, AI has dropped Cost of Proof toward zero.

**Lovable example.** Building a working prototype used to require a development team and months of work. Now: a prompt. The Cost of Proof for *"can this tool build what I need?"* went from **tens of thousands of dollars to ~zero**. That's an entirely new category of product-led businesses that couldn't have existed five years ago.

> If your objection last year was "we can't possibly let people experience our product without a guided setup," it's worth asking that question again. **The answer might have changed.**

## Why It Replaces the SaaS/Non-SaaS Line

In the 1st edition (2019) the line was drawn at SaaS vs. non-SaaS. That was a proxy. The actual variable is the economic one: how much does it cost to deliver proof?

- ProductLed (consulting) → Cost of Proof = preparing and delivering a high-value session. Not zero, but viable. **Still product-led.**
- A cologne brand → Cost of Proof = pennies. Naturally product-led.
- A factory automation system → Cost of Proof = potentially six figures per proof. PLG probably not the move *yet*.

## Strategic Question

> Will your Cost of Proof come down? Almost certainly, yes.
> Will *you* be the one who figures out how to lower it, or will a competitor do it first?
