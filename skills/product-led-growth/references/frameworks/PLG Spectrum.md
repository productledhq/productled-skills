# The PLG Spectrum

> At its core, PLG is simple: **let people buy the way they want to buy.**

PLG isn't binary. It's a spectrum from fully sales-led to fully self-serve. Where you land should be a strategic choice, not an accident.

```
SALES-LED ──────── HYBRID PRODUCT-LED SALES ──────── PURE PLG
   $50K+ ACV              $5K–$50K ACV              <$5K ACV
   Salesforce               Zoom, Notion              Canva (early)
   Workday                  Miro                      Figma (early)
   Palantir                                           Loom, Grammarly
```

## Sales-Led Growth

| | |
|---|---|
| **Best for** | Complex enterprise, ACV $50K+ |
| **Why** | High-stakes problems requiring deep discovery, customization, multi-stakeholder alignment |
| **Sales role** | Drives the entire motion. Relationships built early, value communicated via conversations/demos/proposals long before users touch the product |
| **Examples** | Salesforce, Workday, Palantir |

## Hybrid Product-Led Sales

| | |
|---|---|
| **Best for** | Products serving multiple segments, ACV $5K–$50K |
| **Why** | Most common destination as companies scale |
| **How** | Product leads early engagement. Sales steps in based on user behavior, intent, complexity. SMB self-serves; mid-market gets sales-assisted; enterprise gets traditional sales after product has demonstrated value |
| **Examples** | Zoom, Notion, Miro |

> ⚠️ **Don't try to start here.** To get to your first $1M, choose between sales-led or product-led *before* layering on a hybrid motion. Without a real PLG foundation, hybrid collapses back into sales-led.

## Pure PLG

| | |
|---|---|
| **Best for** | Simple products with low ACVs, <$5K |
| **Why** | Buying decision is low-risk; product is intuitive; scale comes from volume, not deal size |
| **How** | Users discover → experience value → upgrade → expand without speaking to a salesperson |
| **Examples (entry)** | Canva, Figma, Loom, Grammarly |

> Very few companies stay purely PLG as they grow. **Large companies still want the easiest tools to use**, so PLG entry plus enterprise expansion is the pattern. Even Canva has layered in sales for larger teams.

## When Sales Adds Value vs. Friction

### ✅ Sales adds value when complexity is real
**VMware rep example.** His job was advising on which combination of products would solve the client's goal. Custom configurations. Millions on the line: if a piece went down, people lost their jobs. **Constructing solutions, not taking orders.** Insanely valuable consultative selling.

> When a sales rep is constructing the solution, they earn their place. When they're processing a transaction the buyer could handle alone, they become overhead.

### ❌ Sales becomes friction when it shows up too early or blocks the path
- **Data enrichment company example.** 3 calls + emails in 15 minutes of a free trial, before any contact was enriched. Wes started looking for alternatives.
- **Client portal example.** "Book a Demo" only option. Nine days to schedule. By then Wes had found and started using an alternative.

> Most sales-led companies don't factor that gap into time-to-value. The clock starts the moment the buyer decides to evaluate, **not when the demo happens.**

## Case Studies of Transformation

| Company | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| **Boomi** ($4B platform) | Forced every buyer through sales | Self-serve entry → **2x** free-to-paid; ~10% (~$400M) new customers from self-serve |
| **Ascora** (field service, est. 2009) | Mandatory paid human onboarding | Reduced friction + AI chat + interactive demos → **~50%** new sales from self-serve |
| **Enzuzo** (privacy, team of 3, Shopify app) | Freemium with weak conversion | Tightened onboarding + clearer free/paid lines → **2x** free-to-paid in 6 months; **~15 min** sales cycle |

> PLG did not replace sales. **It made sales more effective.**

## The Single Question

> Do people want to buy the way you're selling?

97% of B2B buyers want to try before they buy. Align your motion with that preference, instead of fighting it.
