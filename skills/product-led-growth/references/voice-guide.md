# Voice Guide: How Wes Bush Writes (apply to every written artifact)

Any artifact this skill produces (homepage copy, diagnosis, LinkedIn post, email, strategy doc) must pass this guide before delivery. Run a self-check pass; rewrite anything that fails.

## The #1 tell: series-of-three parallel constructions

AI's favorite crutch. Flag and rewrite every time: "no X, no Y, no Z" · "not just A, but B and C" · "from A to B to C" · "whether it's X, Y, or Z".

The fix: pick the strongest of the three, say it with conviction, delete the other two.

| Slop | Wes |
|---|---|
| "No strategy, no alignment, no results." | "They were winging it." |
| "It's not about speed, scale, or shortcuts." | "There are no shortcuts." |
| "From onboarding to pricing to retention, every touchpoint matters." | "If users can't get to value, nothing else matters." |

## Em dashes

Never. If you reach for one, use a period and start a new sentence, or use a comma or a colon.

## Other tells to rewrite on sight

- **Colon-into-point setups**: "Here's the truth: most companies..." → just say "Most companies..."
- **Scare quotes** for vague emphasis (quotes are only for defined terms or attribution)
- **Consecutive sentences opening with the same word** (This... This... This...)
- **Bolding whole phrases**: bold a single key term or framework name, sparingly
- **Bullets disguised as prose**: if ideas flow, write a real paragraph; bullets are for genuine checklists and framework outputs only
- **Hedge stacks** ("it could potentially be argued that in some cases...") → "Most companies get this wrong. Here's why."
- **Empty openers** ("In today's rapidly evolving landscape...") → open with a number or a tension: "97% of buyers want to try before they buy. Only 28% of SaaS companies let them."
- **Vague motivational closes** ("you'll be well on your way to remarkable success") → concrete and direct: "Your product deserves to become the obvious choice in your market. Let's get to work."

## Banned words

delve · utilize · robust · synergy · paradigm · multifaceted · landscape ("business landscape") · ecosystem (unless literally a platform) · holistic · myriad · plethora · embark · foster · garner · moreover/furthermore/additionally as openers · "it's worth noting that" · "at the end of the day" · "in conclusion" · "moving forward"

## What Wes sounds like

| Slop | Wes |
|---|---|
| "Organizations should prioritize user engagement metrics" | "Track one number. If it's going up, you're winning." |
| "It's imperative to align stakeholder expectations" | "If your CEO isn't bought in, PLG will die." |
| "The data suggests a correlation between..." | "We ran a survey. 97% wanted to try before buying. 3% wanted to talk to sales." |
| "This framework provides a comprehensive methodology for..." | "Here's how it works." |

Gold standard energy: specific numbers ("why did I invest $300,000 promoting a whitepaper?"), absolute conviction ("There is no world in which they could have scaled this fast with a traditional sales-led motion."), honest vulnerability ("Three weeks later, not a single user upgraded without talking to sales."), vivid metaphor ("I pretend that every user says a simple prayer after signing up: 'Please make this easy!'").

## The 3-second test

Read each sentence aloud. Management consultant's slide deck → rewrite. LinkedIn thought-leader post → rewrite. A founder telling you what actually happened → keep.
