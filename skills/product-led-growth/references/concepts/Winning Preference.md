# Winning Preference

> The moment a user experiences a product and **immediately knows the old way of doing things is obsolete.**

## Definition

Not a gradual comparison. Not a feature checklist. **An instant, irreversible shift** in the user's perception of every competing product.

> Once someone touches a PLG 2.0 product, they don't run a feature comparison or negotiate pricing. They just can't go back. The old way feels outdated.

## Wes's Gamma Moment

> "I started using Gamma, and within five minutes, I knew I was never opening Google Slides again. It wasn't a decision. **The old way felt broken the moment I experienced the new way.**"

## Why It Matters

This is what separates **steady growth** from **breakout growth.**

> It's not just product-market fit. It's **product-market lock-in at first touch.**

This is the engine behind every viral [[PLG 1.0, 2.0, 3.0|PLG 2.0]] company. They're not winning by being marginally better. They're winning by making everything else feel broken.

## How to Earn It (the PLG 2.0 prescription)

The output must be **good enough to win preference on first touch.** And the way you do that:

> **The homepage is the product itself.**

Look at how ChatGPT, Claude, Lovable open. The product is right there. You can start getting value before you've created an account.

| | PLG 1.0 design | PLG 2.0 design |
|---|---|---|
| What you design | The journey (signup, onboarding, activation) | The outcome |
| Activation | Step 5 | First touchpoint |
| Question | "Did they convert?" | "Was the first output good enough they'll never look back?" |

## What It Isn't

> Founders see this and think "we need to add AI." So they slap a chatbot in the sidebar. An "AI assistant" that auto-completes a single field.

That's not Winning Preference. That's a chatbot stapled to a PLG 1.0 product. The user still does 90% of the work. The workflow didn't change.
