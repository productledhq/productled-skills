# Proof Experience

> The free experience a product-led company offers that **delivers enough value users would pay for it**, not a lead magnet, not a marketing teaser.

## Definition

A Proof Experience is the mechanism by which a user experiences your value before they pay. It's the answer to "what's the product?" in a product-led company, and **the format varies by industry.**

- SaaS → free trial / freemium
- Service business (e.g., ProductLed) → free IP + high-value consultative sessions
- Education company → a free course genuinely good enough to stand on its own
- Cologne brand → a spritz from a tester
- Ice cream shop → a sample

## The Test

You can tell it's a Proof Experience if it passes the first part of the [[Product-Led Test]]:

> **If you charged for it, would people still pay for it?**

| Answer | What you're running |
|---|---|
| No | Lead-generation play (lead magnet) |
| Yes | Proof Experience |

## The Feeling Test

When someone goes through a real Proof Experience, they walk away thinking:

> *"That alone was worth paying for."*

They don't feel funneled or teased. They feel like **they've already gotten something meaningful, and now they want more.**

Contrast: a PDF that collects your email but teaches you nothing is marketing with a gate on it.

## Canva's Proof Experience

> The Proof Experience isn't browsing templates. **It's actually designing something** using the platform and producing a high-quality design.

You're not looking around. You're **producing.** By design.

## ProductLed's Proof Experience

> "The call wasn't the pitch. **The call was the product.**"

Every ProductLed client first reads the book, uses the frameworks, often implements pieces before they speak to anyone. By the time they show up to a growth session, they aren't being sold. They're being shown exactly how their business would scale, with a real audit and real recommendations.

The session itself delivers value worth paying for. *That's* the Proof Experience.

## Two Constraints

1. **Value test**: would people pay for it? ([[Product-Led Test]])
2. **Economics test**: can you afford to deliver it? ([[Cost of Proof]])

Both can be product-led. A SaaS with ~$0 Cost of Proof offers it to everyone. A consulting firm with higher Cost of Proof qualifies first, then delivers. The format differs. **The principle is the same: value before payment.**
