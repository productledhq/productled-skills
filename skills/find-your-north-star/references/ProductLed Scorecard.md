# ProductLed Scorecard

> A simple spreadsheet your entire team can easily view and contribute to. **The output of the [[True North Framework]].**

## What's On It

All metrics identified in the [[True North Framework]]:

1. **North Star Metric (NSM)**: one metric aligning the company
2. **6 Go-to-Market metrics**: visits · signups · setup · [[First Strike]] · [[Key Usage Indicator (KUI)|KUI]] · upgrade
3. **3 Business Health metrics**: MRR · Total Revenue Churn · Cash Balance

Plus optional add-ons (NRR, Expansion MRR, ARPU, CAC), *just enough to know what's going on, like car dashboard gauges.*

## The Ownership Layer

Every metric gets an **owner.** Owner must pass the **EOS® Ownership Test:**
- Will they make a big impact on this metric?
- Do they *want* to own it?
- Do they have the *capacity*?

> "Right Person in the Right Seat."

A dedicated owner ensures each part of the customer journey has someone responsible. Weekly cadence ensures it stays top priority.

## Backfill Targets

Before assigning random monthly targets for new owners:
1. **Backfill the previous month** of data
2. Identify *where* they can track it
3. Confirm they *can* track it
4. Establish a realistic baseline

## Why Manually Filled

> By design, the scorecard is filled **manually.** Entering the data yourself encourages deeper thinking about why a metric is off or how to better achieve a goal.

Even if data lives in a complex warehouse, the act of pulling and typing it forces engagement.

## The Five-Status System

In every [[Predictable Growth Process|SAM and MFM]], each owner marks their metric:

1. **On Track**
2. **Trending On Track**
3. **Off Track, Have a Plan** *(link the plan, create an issue, review it)*
4. **Off Track, No Plan** → Issue
5. **Off Track, No Plan, Target Unreachable** → Issue

> Great leaders showcase ownership by creating a plan of attack when a metric is off. **Peer-to-peer accountability at the highest level.**

## "CEO for the Week"

> Whoever owns an off-track metric becomes **"CEO for the week."** Their metric becomes everyone's top priority. They can enlist the full support of the company.

Over time → collaborative culture + relentless attack on bottlenecks.

## Why "Less Is More"

> ProductLed's first scorecard had **36 metrics** for a team of 5. Spread thin. Little progress.

> Every new metric **waters down focus.** Resist the urge.
