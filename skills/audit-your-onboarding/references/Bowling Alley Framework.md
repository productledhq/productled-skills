# Bowling Alley Framework

> **40–60% of users who sign up for your product never return.** It's a bloodbath.

## The Metaphor

In bowling, gutters on either side eat your ball. Phil Kinzer invented **bumper bowling**: bumpers keep the ball out of the gutters. **Even a newbie can get a strike.**

> When users get sidetracked, **bump them back** in the right direction. Add bumpers to your product experience.

**Snappa MRR: +20% in one week** by deploying this framework.

> As you eliminate challenges, your TAM expands: more people can experience your product's value.

## Three Phases

```
       PHASE 1                    PHASE 2                  PHASE 3
       Straight Line              Product Bumpers          Conversational Bumpers
       ─────────────              ─────────────             ─────────────────
       Map → Profile →            Welcome Messages          External Messaging
       Label (G/Y/R) →            Product Tours             Knowledge Base
       Define                     Progress Bars             In-App Messaging
                                  Checklists                Community Forums
                                  Tooltips                  Training
                                  Empty States              Specialists
```

## Phase 1: Build Your Straight Line

> The shortest distance from Point A to Point B. **Most users never make it to Point B.**

> **Canva** customized onboarding for specific use cases ("how to make a poster") → cut TTV in half.

### Step 1: Map the Fast Path

> **50% of required onboarding steps are rubbish.** (Yours too.)

Map every step from Visit → [[First Strike]]. Even small "OK" buttons count. **Screenshot every step.**

> Why not map to upgrade right away? **40–60% drop at First Strike.** Biggest impact lives there.
> Second pass: extend to [[Key Usage Indicator (KUI)|KUI]]. Third pass: to Upgrade.

### Step 2: Add Profiling Questions

> Add more steps? Yes, if they personalize the journey.

Two question types:
1. What identifies an ideal user vs. not?
2. What lets us catapult users to value?

**Monday.com asks 5 questions** at signup (use case · role · company size · area to manage · focus). Within 5 questions, they know if you're an ideal user + drop you into the right template.

**ChannelReply asks 2:** Where are you selling online? + What help desk do you use?

### Step 3: Label Each Step

| Color | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 🟢 **Green** | Absolutely necessary (JS install, email for account) | Keep |
| 🟡 **Yellow** | Advanced features that can be delayed (custom signature, A/B tests) | Delay |
| 🔴 **Red** | Can be removed completely (profile color, nickname) | Remove |

> **Yellow is the hardest to label.** Usually necessary, but not *now*.

### Step 4: Define the Straight Line

> Wes's Hamilton bus story: Main Street installed a "green light sequence." Hit one green light → keep hitting greens. **25% faster commute.**

Your straight line should feel like an **unbroken string of greens.**

Discuss with people from product, engineering, marketing, sales.

> Companies consistently **kill 30–40% of steps**, delay another 20%, thrive with what's left.
> A straight-line onboarding experience makes it **2–3× easier** for users to experience core value.

## Phase 2: Product Bumpers

Keep users in the lane while in the app.

| Bumper | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Welcome Messages** | Greet, restate value prop, set expectations |
| **Product Tours** | Eliminate distractions; powerful for multi-product apps; profile + launch into relevant area |
| **Progress Bars** | Set expectation for steps remaining (motivation) |
| **Checklists** | Break big tasks into small ones. **Pre-fill items** to maximize value perception |
| **Onboarding Tooltips** | Help users learn the product. *Don't drown them in a sea of tooltips* |
| **Empty States** | Prompt users on a blank dashboard to take a value-leading action |

> Don't use all of them. Use only where users drop off (revealed by the [[True North Framework]]).

> Not uncommon to **double the number of users who reach value and upgrade** after installing product bumpers.

## Phase 3: Conversational Bumpers

> If someone signs up but never sets foot in the product, product bumpers can't reach them. **Conversational bumpers can.**

Four roles:
1. Educate users
2. Meet them where they are; pull back into product
3. Increase motivation
4. Assist where the product can't

### The Care Continuum (hands-off → hands-on)

> Full atomic note: [[Care Continuum]]

| Bumper | Hands-on level | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **External Messaging** | Most hands-off | Email, SMS, LinkedIn, snail mail (catches users wherever they spend time) |
| **Knowledge Base** | Hands-off, self-serve | Often paired with in-app chat for instant answers |
| **In-App Messaging** | Live human response | Specific questions get near-instant in-app answers |
| **Community Forums** | User-driven | Notion templates, etc. |
| **Training** | Structured education | Weekly coaching, academies, cohorts |
| **Specialists** | Most hands-on | Tech support specialists, sales specialists, onboarding specialists |

> Some bumpers won't be sustainable depending on LTV (you can't assign every $10/mo customer a specialist).

> **Optimize for hands-on at the beginning** to accelerate learning. Scale down once you know what works. Don't be a "helicopter parent" doing everything. Act as a coach.

## The VIP Onboarding Tactic

If it's your first time, don't implement all six bumpers. Roll out the **VIP experience.**

Give every signup (e.g., all 40 this week) white-glove treatment:
- Welcome email
- Onboarding call
- Trial extension
- Manual account setup
- Everything possible

> Once you identify a winning pattern, **reverse-engineer it into scalable bumpers.**

Even at thousands of signups/week, keep VIP onboarding for high-value users. That's how your team learns what users actually need.

> **Do the unscalable to learn what makes users successful. Then reverse-engineer the scalable path.**
