# Care Continuum

> The six types of **conversational bumpers** in the [[Bowling Alley Framework]], ranged from hands-off to hands-on.

If a user signs up but never sets foot in your product, **product bumpers can't reach them. Conversational bumpers can.**

Four roles of conversational bumpers:
1. Educate users
2. Meet them where they are; pull back into product
3. Increase motivation
4. Assist where the product can't

## The Six Tiers

```
hands-off  ◄═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════►  hands-on

1. External    2. Knowledge   3. In-App      4. Community   5. Training    6. Specialists
   Messaging      Base           Messaging       Forums
   (email, SMS,   (self-serve    (live in-app    (Notion         (cohorts,      (sales/onboarding
   LinkedIn,      answers)       chat)           templates)      academies)     specialists)
   snail mail)
```

### 1. External Messaging
Email, SMS, LinkedIn connection requests, even snail mail. **Catches users wherever they spend their time**. They won't log in every day at the start. Best ones include clear next steps.

### 2. Knowledge Base
Often paired with in-app chat. **Instantaneous answers to standard questions.** Empowers users to solve problems independently. Deflects support tickets.

### 3. In-App Messaging
> Sometimes you're struggling with something and need to ask a very specific question. It's incredible to message in-app and get a **near-instant solution from a live representative.** Even with a few-hour delay, it signals a team that cares.

### 4. Community Forums
Communities serve new users in better and different ways than support.

> **Notion**: community where users share templates simultaneously increases adoption, engagement, and retention. *A powerful combo.*

### 5. Training
> Most software requires expertise. You can make it incredibly easy to use, but you're missing a massive opportunity if you don't address the **knowledge gap.**

Forms: weekly coaching calls · academies · live cohorts.

### 6. Specialists
**Most hands-on.**
- Technical Support Specialists: easier hit on the [[First Strike]]
- Sales Specialists: help users make the business case to upgrade

> Speed up time to value and upgrade. **One of the easiest to start rolling out.**

## Pricing Reality

> Depending on average LTV, some bumpers won't be sustainable. *(Can't assign every $10/mo customer a specialist.)*

That said, **optimize for hands-on at the beginning** to accelerate learning.

## The VIP Strategy

> Start hands-on. Scale down once you know what works.

If first time:
1. Don't implement all six bumpers
2. Roll out **VIP experience**: white-glove every signup (welcome email, onboarding call, trial extension, manual setup)
3. Identify the winning pattern → **reverse-engineer it into scalable bumpers** *(e.g., manual welcome email → automated; common onboarding-call answers → knowledge base)*

> **Do the unscalable to learn what makes users successful. Then reverse-engineer the scalable path.**

Even at thousands of signups/week, keep VIP onboarding for high-value users. That's how your team learns what users actually need.

## Coach, Not Helicopter Parent

> Don't let your conversational bumpers feel like a helicopter parent doing everything for the child. That turns away motivated users. **Act as a coach**: guidance to keep them on track when needed.
