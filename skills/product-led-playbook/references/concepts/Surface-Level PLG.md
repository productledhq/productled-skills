# Surface-Level PLG

> The #1 failure mode of PLG initiatives. **Treating PLG as a product thing instead of a company-wide strategy.**

## The Symptom

Companies starting their PLG rollout typically:
- Launch a free trial / freemium
- Build transparent pricing
- Improve onboarding for faster TTV
- Update the homepage for more signups

> All important. **But they're only half the story.**

## The Cascade

When PLG is rolled out without a [[Product-Led Organization (PLO)]]:

1. **No organizational buy-in**
2. **Lackluster results**
3. **Unfocused execution**
4. **Not enough value before monetizing**

> Businesses doing surface-level PLG often **kill the motion entirely** because it doesn't work.

## The Iceberg

Above the waterline = the PLG motion (signup, onboarding, pricing, homepage).
Below the waterline = the [[Product-Led Organization (PLO)|PLO]] that makes PLG actually work: strategy, ideal user clarity, user behavior visibility, weekly rhythms, growth process, elite team.

> *"What we lacked at Vidyard was everything below the surface that supports PLG."*

## The Vidyard Story (Full Version)

In 2016, Vidyard tried PLG. Some competitors had a free trial → seemed like a good idea. They created a landing page, funneled visitors to it, gave them access.

- Initial surge of signups
- 3 weeks later: **zero upgrades without sales**
- Team wondered if something was wrong with the product
- *Hit the hidden part of the iceberg at full speed. Killed the trial.*

When Wes tried to rally key leaders to fix it:
> "the company had other priorities. It wasn't on the roadmap."

## Why It Happens

The book itself is called *"Product-Led Growth"*. The word "product" is right there. Easy to think it's a product team initiative.

> When done right, PLG aligns how your **entire company** creates and captures value. Without organizational buy-in and a unified strategy, you risk treating PLG as just another product feature rather than a company-wide growth engine.

> **PLG is a go-to-market (GTM) motion, not a product strategy.**

## The Data Point

Wes manually analyzed 324 ProductLed clients:
- 80% were senior product execs. <20% were SaaS founders.
- **But the tiny 20% of founders generated 80% of results.**

Why? When founders led PLG, it was treated as a **company-wide initiative**, not just product team.

## The Fix

Stop optimizing the PLG motion in isolation. **Build the PLO underneath it.** That's what the entire [[ProductLed System]] does.

> Instead of asking "how do we optimize our PLG?" ask:
> **"How can I design an organization so that PLG succeeds?"**
