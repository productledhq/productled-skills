# Product-Led Organization (PLO)

> **The company-focused backbone that makes PLG happen.**

## Definition

> **PLG is everything the user interacts with on the front end. PLO ensures the company is aligned and firing on all cylinders on the back end.**

> **Becoming product-led is the integration of both.**

## What a PLO Needs

You can't deploy PLG without a PLO. Required ingredients:

1. **A company-level strategy** in which PLG is a key part of winning in the market
2. **Clarity on your ideal user** *(most sales-led businesses know the buyer well, but who's the user?)*
3. **A real view of what users actually do in the product**
4. **Weekly rhythms to identify bottlenecks**
5. **An effective growth process to launch regular experiments**
6. **An elite team with the right capabilities**

## The Vidyard Failure Mode

Vidyard tried PLG in 2016 without a PLO. Surge of signups → three weeks later, **zero upgrades without sales.** When Wes approached leaders to fix it: *"the company had other priorities. It wasn't on the roadmap."*

That's the [[Surface-Level PLG]] trap: launching PLG without the back-end org structure that makes it work.

## How the [[ProductLed System]] Builds a PLO

| Stage | Builds | Maps to PLO need |
|---|---|---|
| **Stage 1** Foundation | Strategy · User · Model | Company-level strategy · Ideal user clarity |
| **Stage 2** Self-Serve | Offer · Onboarding · Pricing | (the PLG front-end users touch) |
| **Stage 3** Expansion | Data · Process · Team | What users do in the product · Weekly rhythms · Growth process · Elite team |

So Stages 1 and 3 are largely PLO. Stage 2 is largely PLG. **You need all three.**

## The Reframe

> Instead of focusing solely on optimizing your PLG motion, ask:
> **"How can I design an organization so that PLG succeeds?"**

This is the question the entire Playbook answers.
