# Elite Team Flywheel

> **Userflow hit $5M ARR with just 3 employees. No contractors.** Over **$1.6M RPE.**

> **In a product-led business, your product is the machine, not your team.** Hire as a last resort: by the time you've nailed the prior components, the product solves most problems.

## Four Phases

```
PHASE 1 ──► Design the Team
              Accountability Chart · Visionary/Integrator · 3T System
                     │
PHASE 2 ──► Audit the Team
              Quick Audit (Values) · Performance Audit (MRSC)
                     │
PHASE 3 ──► Incentivize Peak Performance
              5-Year Vision · Shared Stakes
                     │
PHASE 4 ──► Develop Unstoppable Capabilities
              Monthly 1:1 · One Skill · One Resource · Coach
```

## Phase 1: Design the Team

### The Accountability Chart
Visual tool defining roles, responsibilities, and reporting relationships.

> Difference from an org chart: **one person can own more than one job.** Your Head of Product might also be Head of Finance + website manager.

Without breaking each job out, you hire expensive unicorns. (Finding someone great at social media + ops + finance is much harder than finding someone for just social.)

> *Traction* by Gino Wickman introduced this. ProductLed found underperformance was almost always because someone was doing too many jobs.

### Top-Down Design

**EOS® unbundles the CEO into two roles:**

| Role | What |
|---|---|
| **Visionary** | Creative ideas, sets vision, values, strategy, culture |
| **Integrator** | COO/CTO-type, systematic, turns vision into reality |

Solo founders wear both hats. As you grow, these usually become separate. **Very few people can be a true Visionary AND brilliant Integrator.**

Below leadership: typically **Head of GTM** + **Head of Product** (the two most important PLG areas).

> Don't get caught up in titles. What matters is the responsibilities. Keeps ego out of it.

Ensure the core capabilities from your [[One-Page Endgame (OPE)|OPE]] are well represented.

### The 3T System (Mike Michalowitz, *Clockwork*)

For each job, decide:
- **Trash**: safely eliminate; nothing breaks
- **Transfer**: important, but someone else can do it better
- **Treasure**: critical; protect + dedicate more time

> Eliminate **10–20% of jobs** that are no longer required. Like a sports team, each role is intentional. Three goalies is silly. How often do you have 2–3 people doing one specialist's job?

Review accountability chart during every SAM.

## Phase 2: Audit the Team

Like a virus scan: **quick scan + full performance scan.** Always run the quick first.

### Quick Audit (Core Values)

Use your [[One-Page Endgame (OPE)|OPE]] core values as criteria. Score each person:
- **+** Always
- **+/-** Sometimes
- **-** Never

Use it during interviews, in the first weeks after hiring, before probationary period ends, when fit is in question.

> **Example (values: Simplicity / Energy / Drive):**
> - **Sarah:** -, -, +/- → likely let go
> - **Josh:** +/-, +/-, +/- → mediocre across the board → let go
> - **Melissa:** +, +/-, + → almost perfect; coach on energy

You might add non-negotiables like Integrity.

### Performance Audit: MRSC (per job)

Use when:
- Underperformance
- Raising the bar proactively
- Someone owns >3 jobs

For each job, score on:
1. **M**otivation: do they want this job?
2. **R**esults: do they consistently hit targets?
3. **S**kills: do they have the required skills?
4. **C**apacity: do they have the time?

> **Melissa example:**
> | Job | M | R | S | C |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | Social Media Mgmt | + | + | + | - |
> | Copywriter | + | +/- | - | - |
> | Email Marketer | - | + | + | - |
>
> Move email marketing to a more motivated team member. Coach copywriting (she wants to improve). Free up capacity for social.

> Before auditing your team, **audit yourself first.** Odds are you're doing too many jobs and aren't the right person for all of them.

## Phase 3: Incentivize Peak Performance

### Individuals: Unlock Motivation

> Ask each direct report: **"What is your 5-year vision?"**

- What do you want to be earning?
- What do you want to be doing?
- Ideal work/life balance?
- What does success look like?

> When their vision aligns with what they do daily, they stop working *for you* and start working *for themselves*. You unlock a whole new level of productivity.

Tie all critique back to their vision: how improvement gets them there faster.

> Sometimes a clear vision doesn't align with the company. You may have to part ways, but you'll know why and can create a transition that works for everyone.

### Team: Shared Stakes

> Most companies hit their endgame and the founder is the only one who notices. **Elite teams love to win.** Design shared stakes.

Examples:
- Profit target $1M → 10% in a team profit pool
- Markers of progress: at 1K customers, company retreat; at 5K, recurring incentive
- Recurring: profit sharing

> What makes winning great is something is at stake. If you don't win, you lose out. If you win, you unlock something new.

Mastering incentives:
1. Aligns team with company success
2. Increases engagement
3. Improves retention
4. Attracts top talent
5. Boosts collaboration

## Phase 4: Develop Unstoppable Capabilities

> Capabilities from your OPE **can't be outsourced.** They must be built in-house.

> Team development is one of the most underrated leadership skills. Passive, ad-hoc "development" won't take a team from great to elite.

Use **monthly one-on-ones (30 min each).**

### Develop Each Individual

First 1:1 of the month: identify **one** critical area to develop.

If they don't know where to start, ask:
1. How did you do last month qualitatively? What can you improve?
2. Here are your metrics. What do you see?
3. What skill to work on this month? How can I help?

### Identify One High-Impact Resource

Book · course · certification. **Type doesn't matter, effectiveness does.**

> Ideally the individual picks (more motivation). Or recommend a proven resource. The value is committing to a resource → clear "done/not done" verdict.

If you both need it, do it together. *(Wes + Laura took Write Useful Books together while writing this book.)*

### Coach the Development

At each weekly 1:1, ask how training is going:
- Acts as trigger to prioritize it
- Shows you take their development seriously
- If you have advanced skills in the topic, coach with examples → accelerates faster than anything

Make completion a personal project: decide in MFMs, review in WAMs.

## Long-Term Defensibility

> Eventually your product will be copied. So will your marketing. So will your experience. **Your long-term differentiator is your team, and how you improve it.**

> Even as competitors poach talent, **they can't poach the high-performance culture that cultivates it.**
