# Bullseye Strategy Framework

> **A strategy is not a plan; it's a theory on how you will win.**

Adapted from Roger Martin & A.G. Lafley's *Playing to Win* (clunky for SaaS, so Wes simplified it). **Five steps** that underpin your high-level strategy.

## The 5 Steps

```
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ 1. Which market can you dominate as the Obvious Choice? │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. Where will you play?                                │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. How will you win? (Moats)                           │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 4. What is your winning picture?                       │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 5. What strategic choices must you make?               │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Step 1: Which Market Can You Dominate as the [[Obvious Choice]]?

Every market becomes commoditized. In a commoditized market, **it's a race to become the obvious choice.**

There's only one Canva. One HubSpot. One Atlassian. One Zoom.

The four [[Market Ascent (5 Levels)|Market Ascent]] zones apply here too:
- **Commodity**: product doesn't stand out; price competition; users need hand-holding
- **Contender**: differentiated, but still hustle-driven
- **Cusp**: top 3–5; 7-figure profits; competitors copy fast
- **Chosen**: the Canva of your industry

> Don't try to beat HubSpot at all SMB CRM. Be the best CRM for **real estate agents**.

### Obvious Choice Statement (template)

> *We are the obvious choice for [SEGMENT] who struggle with [SPECIFIC PROBLEM].*

**Paubox:** "We are the obvious choice for mental health professionals who struggle with HIPAA email compliance."

## Step 2: Where Will You Play?

Five questions:
1. **Ideal customer**: group that loves your product as-is
2. **Main problem**: the meaningful problem you address
3. **Core product**: the product that drives most revenue
4. **Primary marketing channels**: main channels generating traffic + signups (read *Traction* by Weinberg & Mares)
5. **Geography**: regions with the most ideal customers

> Be specific. An army fighting multiple battles always loses to one focused on a single beachhead.

**Paubox example:**

| Field | Choice |
|---|---|
| Ideal Customer | Mental health professional at a single location |
| Main Problem | HIPAA email compliance |
| Core Product | Email suite |
| Primary Marketing Channels | Organic Search |
| Geography | United States |

## Step 3: How Will You Win? ([[15 Moats|Moats]])

> A **moat** = business defensibility. Like the moat around Chiang Mai. Defenders gain two advantages: cross water + climb walls.

You can and should build more than one moat. **Ideally three.** A competitor might copy one, but copying three is nearly impossible.

> Full catalog: [[15 Moats]]

### Four common moat combinations

| Strategy | Moat combo |
|---|---|
| Low-cost leader | Cost Leadership + Operational Efficiency |
| Go upmarket | Differentiation + Customer Experience + High Switching Costs |
| Dominant market leader | Cost Leadership + Operational Efficiency + UX |
| Disrupt stagnant industry | Free Moat + Speed + Cost Leadership *(Canva vs. Photoshop)* |

> Each moat complements others. Together = unstoppable combination.

**Don't build too many simultaneously.** Start with 1–2; once built, add more.

> A moat needs maintenance. Differentiation Moat with a couple unique features → competitor copies them → moat evaporates as customers see fewer differences.

## Step 4: What Is Your Winning Picture?

> If your team has a different definition of winning, what are the odds you'll win? **Slim to none.**

NFL has Super Bowl. Cyclists have Tour de France. Tennis has Wimbledon. **You need an endgame.**

### Endgame: SMART (5–10 years out)

Examples:
- Reach $100M revenue by end of 2040
- Exit for $60M in next 5 years
- Serve 10,000 customers in 5 years
- 100% employee-owned by 2032
- 1M engaged users by 2030

### Cascade into Pictures

**1-Year Picture**
- Date (Dec 31, year)
- Top Outcomes: Revenue · RPE · Profit
- 3 Top Annual Goals (SMART)
- Reward (optional)

**Quarterly Picture**
- Top Outcomes: Revenue · Profit
- 3 Top Quarterly Goals (support Annual)
- Reward

**Monthly Picture**: *focus drives massive progress*
- Top Outcomes
- **One** Top Focus
- Top Monthly Projects (resources aligned)
- Reward

> "Great years come from great quarters. Great quarters come from great months. Great months come from great weeks."

## Step 5: What Strategic Choices Must You Make?

> What you don't pursue is as important as what you do.

### Core Values
Only 23% of employees believe in their org's core values. **Hire AND fire on them.** Aim for 3. Less is more.

If stuck: ask what bothers you about how some companies treat you. Your values may be the *opposite*.

### Capabilities: How You Create Your Moats
Two people with the same how-to-win strategy: **the one who develops the right capabilities wins.**

Drill-down questions:
1. What new capabilities do we need?
2. What existing ones to double down on?
3. **What existing ones are no longer needed?** *(Hardest. Carrying extra weight is a disservice to talent who could thrive elsewhere.)*

Paubox: winning with incredible customer service → need capability to offer incredible support at all times, in all forms, to all users.

### Supplemental Strategic Choices: what to say no to

1. **What must we say no to based on where-to-play?**
   *(SMB-focused CEO kept signing enterprise customers: 95% of support issues, 90% of dev time on custom contracts. Was sabotaging his own company.)*

2. **What must we double down on to lean into how-to-win?**
   *(Paubox: lead with email suite, not forms or texting products.)*

3. **What must we do to achieve our 1-Year Picture?**
   Should you kill initiatives? Reallocate resources? Cut capabilities?

> Strategic choices become your anchor when new initiatives pop up. No ambiguity about where to focus → faster decisions.

## Rolling Out: The One-Page Endgame ([[One-Page Endgame (OPE)|OPE]])

Compress everything into one page. Share with everyone who:
- Has to act on it
- Will be affected by changes
- Could offer valuable feedback

> James A. Robinson & Daron Acemoglu (*Why Nations Fail*): any new change requires creative destruction. **Let there be creative destruction.**

> Jason Fried (37signals): the CEO's main job is to **introduce risk** into the business. Everyone else's job is to manage and mitigate it.

> Your strategy is a theory on how you'll win. **If you have 100% confidence, you waited too long.** Your competitors know it too.

Review the OPE in every SAM (see [[Predictable Growth Process]]).
