# True North Framework

> Imagine you're on the beach, debating extending your vacation. You check your phone. A simple scorecard shows a few key numbers. **If they're solid, you know everything's on track.** If one flashes red, time to act.

This is the power of True North: distill your business down to its critical metrics so you know **instantly** whether to relax or intervene.

## Three Categories of Metrics

```
┌────────────────────┐
│ North Star Metric  │  ← one number aligning the entire company
├────────────────────┤
│ GTM Metrics (6)    │  ← user journey
├────────────────────┤
│ Business Health    │  ← MRR · Churn · Cash
└────────────────────┘
```

## North Star Metric (NSM)

> Captures the **core value** your product delivers. **Aligns customer and company needs.** Improvements should also increase revenue.

### Examples

| Company | NSM |
|---|---|
| Intercom | # Customer Interactions / Week |
| Salesforce | # Records Created / Account / Week |
| Zoom | # Hosted Meetings / Week |
| Medium | # Minutes Spent Reading / Week |
| Twilio | # Total Messages Sent / Month |
| DocuSign | # Documents Signed / Week |
| Airbnb | # Nights Booked / Month |
| Uber | # Trips Booked / Week |
| Instagram | # Active People / Day |
| Slack | # Messages / Week |

> **Airbnb:** when someone books a night, the customer, the host, *and* Airbnb all win. **Aligned.**

### Three Components of a Great NSM

1. **Quality metric**: an action in your product (automated or manual)
2. **Quantity metric**: typically a number
3. **Frequency**: cadence that makes sense for your users

For Slack: messages (quality) · number (quantity) · weekly (frequency, matches workweek). Daily would penalize the weekend lulls. Instagram is the opposite: daily makes sense.

> **Amplitude:** "Weekly Learning Users" = shared reports per week viewed by **2+ people.** Proves users find share-worthy insights, not just logging in.

### NSM Criteria
- Easy to measure
- Correlates with the user's endgame
- A behavior best customers do a lot in your product
- Aligns with company strategy

### If You're Stuck
- Your [[Value Metric]] is the leading NSM candidate
- If still stuck, it might be your [[Key Usage Indicator (KUI)|KUI]] (Slack's KUI of 2,000 messages → NSM of messages/week)

Approach it from **both angles:**
- **Company:** what do your highest-LTV customers do consistently?
- **Customer:** what do your best-retained users do consistently?

## Go-to-Market Metrics (6)

Mirror the [[User Endgame Roadmap|user journey]]:

1. Unique visits
2. Signups
3. Users who complete setup
4. Users who complete [[First Strike]]
5. Users who complete [[Key Usage Indicator (KUI)|KUI]]
6. Users who upgrade

> One client: **7,300 monthly visitors but only 4 signups.** Bottleneck was obvious: signup conversion. Doubling visitors would have solved nothing.

> Track as many as you can. **Resist the urge to add nice-to-haves.** Less is more.

## Business Health Metrics (3)

Nothing else matters if your business runs out of money.

1. **MRR**
2. **Total Revenue Churn**
3. **Cash Balance** ← most overlooked

> ProductLed asked the bookkeeper to track cash weekly. Discovered they were adding expenses at the same rate as revenue. **Cut $300K in annual expenses within 6 months.**

Optional secondary BHMs: NRR · Expansion MRR · ARPU · CAC.

> Treat BHMs like car dashboard gauges. Just enough to know what's going on.

## The ProductLed Scorecard

> Full atomic note: [[ProductLed Scorecard]]

Simple spreadsheet. Every metric gets an **owner** (EOS® Ownership Test):
- Will they make a big impact?
- Do they want to own it?
- Do they have the capacity?

### Backfill Targets
Before assigning random monthly targets, have each owner backfill the previous month:
- Identify *where* they can track the metric
- Confirm they can track it
- Get a realistic baseline

By design, filled out **manually.** Entering data yourself encourages deeper thinking about why a metric is off.

> The owner of an off-track metric becomes **"CEO for the week."** Their metric is the team's top priority: they can enlist the full company. Over time = collaborative culture + relentless ownership.

## Product Qualified Leads (PQLs)

> Full atomic note: [[Product Qualified Lead (PQL)]]

> **Advanced metric.** Start with the scorecard alone for at least a month before adding PQLs.

**PQL formula:**
> **Ideal User Profile + Successful Setup + First Strike + Hit a KUI = PQL**

PQLs have **30–50% odds of upgrading.**

### Rolling Out PQLs: modify the 6 GTM metrics

| Without PQLs | With PQLs |
|---|---|
| Unique visits | Unique visits |
| Signups | **Ideal Signups** |
| Setup | Setup |
| First Strike | First Strike |
| KUI | **PQLs** |
| Upgrade | **Ideal users who upgrade** |

By segmenting on ideal users:
- Marketing is incentivized to drive *ideal* signups
- Customer Success knows who to assist first
- Sales is clear on who's ready to upgrade

## Three Outcomes When You Avoid the Data Traps

1. **Total team alignment**: clarity on what matters
2. **Peer-to-peer accountability**: owners + weekly reviews
3. **Accelerated growth**: focus on biggest bottleneck
