# Six Product-Led Models

> There is **no single correct model.** Multi-billion-dollar PLG companies have made every model work. What goes into the model matters more than the model itself.

## The Six Options

```
       OPT-IN ─── OPT-OUT ─── USAGE-BASED ─── FREEMIUM ─── NEW PRODUCT ─── SANDBOX
       FREE TRIAL FREE TRIAL  FREE TRIAL                                    
       ────────                                                              ────────
       lowest friction                                              last resort
```

---

## 1. Opt-In Free Trial

**What:** Time-based access (7 / 14 / 30 days). No credit card required.

**Why users love it:** Easy to start. No commitment.

**Use when:** Users can experience core value within the time window.

> **Critical question:** Can users find value within the allotted timeframe? *(If not, this model won't work.)*

This is among the most common PLG models. The right starting point for most products that can deliver value quickly.

---

## 2. Opt-Out Free Trial

**What:** Requires credit card to access the free trial.

**Why add the friction?**
1. Reduce spam signups
2. Reduce tire-kickers
3. Set a trigger for users to come back *(education products without natural triggers)*
4. Avoid the cost of setting up a free trial for each user
5. Avoid bogus email signups (relevant for intermittent-use products like Ahrefs SEO tools)
6. Work only with committed users if you're still manually onboarding

> **Last resort.** Adds enormous friction. Typically gets only a fraction of opt-in signups.

> Your free-to-paid conversion rate will look higher, but **only because fewer people signed up in the first place.** Overall, you typically **make less money** than opt-in. The higher conversion doesn't make up for fewer starts.

---

## 3. Usage-Based Free Trial

**What:** Limit by usage instead of time. Specifies how much value you give away.

> **One of Wes's favorites.**

**Examples:**
- **Miro:** 3 boards
- **Vidyard:** 25 videos
- **Evernote:** 50 notes (Wes used the unlimited plan for **8 years** until they switched to a 50-note cap → upgraded in 10 seconds)

**Why it works:**
> Lets users access a limited amount of **all the value** rather than restricting features. The difference between "a limited amount of all the value" vs. "an unlimited amount of some of the value."
>
> Eliminates artificial time pressure. Lets users experience core benefits at their own pace.

---

## 4. Freemium

**What:** Give away certain features forever.

**Examples:** Canva (unlimited free designs, but premium templates / resize / stock photos gated).

**When it works:** When your product requires mastering a specific skill (e.g., design) and ideal users naturally progress from beginner → advanced problems requiring advanced features.

> **The most powerful PLG model**: most compelling offer. Also the **highest-risk** model.

> Rob Walling (former Drip CEO): *"Freemium is like a Samurai sword: unless you're a master at using it, you can cut your arm off."*

**Challenge:** Smaller companies may attract many non-paying users who create distractions.

---

## 5. New Product

> *"This isn't really a new product-led model. You're right."*

When users' biggest blocker means **no existing model will solve the problem**, you may need a new product.

**Vidyard's story (full version):**
- Original blocker: users didn't have a video to upload
- Without a video, they could never see the value
- A longer trial or different model wouldn't help
- Solution: launched the **Chrome extension** to make recording trivial → millions of users since

> When you're not addressing the primary challenge that's holding users back from value, develop a new product that helps users overcome their main challenge faster.

---

## 6. Sandbox

**What:** Clickable product tour with dummy data.

> **Last resort.**

Why? It's not as exciting to play with dummy data and click around. **Nothing replaces seeing your data in a product.** But it can complement other models.

**Where it shines:** Long time-to-value products, products that are difficult to roll out.

Often used to **improve sign-up rates**: users can take a tour first, then sign up for the actual free model.

---

## Choosing the Model

If it's your **first time** launching PLG:
1. **Pick ONE.** Don't be cute with hybrids.
2. Master the basics before going hybrid (e.g., premium trial → freemium downgrade).
3. The right model is the one that lets your users **bridge the gap between where they are and their desired outcome.**

## Cheat Sheet

| Model | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Opt-In Free Trial | Fast time-to-value, broad audience | Users can't reach value in window |
| Opt-Out Free Trial | High-cost-to-serve, sophisticated buyers only | Massive signup drop |
| Usage-Based Free Trial | Value scales with units; no natural time pressure | Defining the right unit |
| Freemium | Skill mastery products; natural progression | Distraction from non-payers; cost to serve |
| New Product | Primary value gap can't be bridged | Building a whole new product is a big bet |
| Sandbox | Long TTV; complex setup; supplementary | Doesn't replace real-data experience |
