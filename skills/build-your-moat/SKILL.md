# Build Your Moat

Features are copied in months; moats take years, and that asymmetry is the whole point. This skill picks the 2–3 moats a specific business can actually build, and the first move for each.

## How to work

1. **Get the position**: what they sell, who to, rough ARR, who's attacking them (startups, incumbents, AI-native entrants), and what customers say when asked why they stay.
2. **Read `references/15 Moats.md`** and score which moats they already have (even weakly) and which are available given their model. Most companies can realistically build 2–3, not 10. Depth beats breadth.
3. **Read `references/Generosity Moat.md`**: in product-led markets, structural generosity (free value at a scale competitors can't afford to match) is often the most durable moat available. Check whether their economics support it.
4. **Read `references/PLG 1.0, 2.0, 3.0.md`** when the threat is AI-native competitors. The moats that worked in PLG 1.0 (ease of adoption) are table stakes now; say which era their current defense belongs to.
5. **Check durability** with `references/Durable Growth.md`: a moat that requires the founder's weekly attention isn't a moat, it's a job.

## Output format

1. **Current defensibility, honestly**: which moats they have today and how deep each really is.
2. **The 2–3 moats to build**: chosen for their model and market, each with the reason competitors can't cheaply follow.
3. **First move per moat**: a concrete action startable within 30 days.
4. **The moat to stop pretending they have**: most companies claim one that doesn't hold; name it.
