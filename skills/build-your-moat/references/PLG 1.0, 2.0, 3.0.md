# PLG 1.0, 2.0, 3.0: The Three Eras

> The PLG that worked in 2019 isn't the same PLG that's winning today. Where your company sits on this curve determines whether you're leading or about to be left behind.

The one variable driving every era: **[[Time-to-Value]].** Each version exists because the previous one was too slow.

| Era | Goal | User Role | TTV | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1.0** User Experience | Shorten path to value | **Builder** | Minutes–hours | Pendo, Appcues, UserGuiding, Userflow, WalkMe (and the products they instrument) |
| **2.0** Output Experience | Collapse path to value | **Editor** | <60 seconds | ChatGPT, Claude, Lovable, Gamma, Cursor, Harvey, Midjourney |
| **3.0** Agent Experience | Eliminate the path entirely | **Orchestrated** (agent decides) | Before you ask | Emerging: Netlify's majority of new signups are now agents |

---

## PLG 1.0: The User Experience Era

> Goal: shorten the path from signup to value.

Tactics: better onboarding, fewer clicks, guided activation. Entire ecosystem built around this (Pendo, Appcues, UserGuiding, Userflow, WalkMe).

**Limitation:** the user still does all the work. The tools just make the work less confusing. **It's a Band-Aid.**

Your users are **Builders**: they need domain expertise to get value. Learn the interface, configure their setup, actively create the output. **TAM is capped at people with the skill and patience to build.**

> PLG 1.0 fundamentals still matter. In tech-laggard industries (healthcare, government), this may be where you should live. **But if you're in a fast-moving industry, most of tech is already at PLG 2.0, and if you don't update, you're toast.**

---

## PLG 2.0: The Output Experience Era

> Goal: collapse time to value. Lead with an AI-generated output the user refines.

> "PLG 1.0 asks 'how do we reduce steps to value.' PLG 2.0 asks **'how do we collapse a user's time to value?'**"

AI-native products **start with an output and ask the user to refine it.** Value isn't at the end of onboarding. It's at the first touch. **<60 seconds.**

### The user shift: Builder → Editor

PLG 2.0 doesn't eliminate the user. **It shifts their role from Builder to Editor.**

- Gamma: you outline what you want to communicate; you review the deck.
- Cursor: you describe the feature; you review the code.
- Lovable: you describe the app; you review/ship.

The heavy lifting of creation, technical execution, and domain expertise → handled by AI. **Your job moves from construction to curation.**

### Why this drives extreme growth: TAM expansion

> There are **10× as many Editors as Builders.**

A founder who can't code can describe an app to Lovable and ship it without a developer. That person was completely locked out before. **PLG 2.0 unlocks an entirely new TAM who couldn't use the old tools.**

Claude Code is not a better dev tool. It turns anyone who can describe what they want into someone who ships software. **TAM isn't "developers." It's "everyone with a software problem."**

### The biggest mistake

Slapping a chatbot in the sidebar and calling it PLG 2.0. The user still does 90% of the work. The workflow didn't change. **That's PLG 1.0 with a chatbot stapled to it.**

### The shift in design philosophy

| | PLG 1.0 | PLG 2.0 |
|---|---|---|
| What you design | The journey | The outcome |
| The homepage | Marketing for the product | **Is the product** |
| Activation | Step 5 of onboarding | The first touchpoint |
| Success metric | Activation rate | First-output quality |

> The homepage **must be the product itself**, like ChatGPT, Claude, Lovable. You can start getting value before creating an account.

## PLG 3.0: The Agent Experience Era

> Difference from 2.0 boils down to one question: **who is the orchestrator?**

| | PLG 2.0 | PLG 3.0 |
|---|---|---|
| Orchestrator | You (the user) | The agent |
| You pick the tools? | Yes (Cursor for code, Gamma for decks…) | No, agent picks |
| Driving metaphor | Advanced autopilot: car is smart, you pick the route | Fully autonomous: you tell it the destination |

Currently emerging. MCP connectors etc. let you use Claude across products in a conversation, but **you're still initiating**. That's still PLG 2.0. The line is crossed when the agent takes over the orchestrator role entirely.

> The majority of new signups on Netlify's platform are now **agents, not humans.** The agent evaluated Netlify and chose it.

### Implications (early)
- Marketing-to-humans matters less; marketing-to-agents matters more (machine-readable docs, MCP servers, evaluation surfaces)
- Brand recall matters less; technical fit + price matters more
- "TAM" extends beyond human users to autonomous agents acting on behalf of users

## Shutterstock as a Cautionary Tale

PLG figured out for 15 years. Browse millions of images, filter, preview, license one-click, instant download. **Frictionless across every step.** Printed money for 15 years.

Then AI image generators showed up. Users stopped browsing entirely. They typed what they wanted and got it. **Shutterstock didn't fail at execution. The entire category was disrupted.**

Same story playing out:
- Google Slides → Gamma
- Stack Overflow → Cursor
- Westlaw → Harvey AI

> Every one of those old-guard companies had great PLG. None of it mattered when a competitor eliminated the **need for a flow in the first place**.

## Diagnostic Question

> *"Are we shortening the path to value, or eliminating it?"*

If the answer is "shortening," you're playing the 1.0 game in a 2.0/3.0 market.
